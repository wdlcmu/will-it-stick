How to write something people remember

Write the line you want repeated. Then earn it.

Four passes over your draft

Pass 1: find the core. Cross out every sentence but one. The survivor is your message; build around it.

Pass 2: swap the fog. Trade each abstract word for a scene. “Soon” becomes “by Friday.” “Help” becomes who does what.

Pass 3: open one gap. Start where the reader leans in: a question, a surprise, a number that begs a second look.

Pass 4: test it cold. Show it to someone who owes you nothing. Ask what they kept; that answer is the grade.

A real check

A real panel run: a status note to a manager. Full note on Examples; only the first line changed.

Before

Status update: The project is progressing.

After

Status update: The project is at risk of delay.

What readers heard

The core: “The project is at risk.” The real need, the manager's help this week, never came through. One line carried the note, and it pointed the wrong way.

The fix asked for one more line, “I need your help this week.” The rewrite never added it.

Where the passes come from

They follow the SUCCESs checklist from Made to Stick, by Chip Heath and Dan Heath. This site is independent; the authors did not endorse it.
