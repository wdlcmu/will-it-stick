Why do people forget what I said?

They rarely forget you. They never got one thing to hold.

Three reasons it slides off

Too many points. Five equal points leave nothing first. Memory keeps one core and blurs the rest.

Big abstract words. “Improve,” “quality,” “going forward”: nothing to picture means nothing to keep. Concrete words stick better (Paivio, 1971).

No open question. A message that answers everything leaves nothing to wonder about. A small gap makes the mind lean in (Loewenstein, 1994).

A real check

A real panel run: a podcast tip to a close friend (excerpt; the full run is on Examples).

Before

You should listen to this podcast I found. It is really interesting and the host is great and talks about a lot of different topics.

What readers heard

“Listen to this interesting podcast.” No name, no scene, no story. Concrete: 1 of 10.

The one fix: “Cut the vague parts. Just say the podcast's name.” The text never named the show; the rewrite could only drop “really.”

The fix, in one line

Say the one thing first, in concrete words. Let the rest support it, not compete.

These checks come from the SUCCESs checklist in Made to Stick, by Chip Heath and Dan Heath. This site is independent; the authors did not endorse it.
