    A work note that hid the risk

    A status update to her manager. The launch was at risk, and the note never says so. Her goal line did, and the readers still scored the note itself 1s and 2s.

    Before“Status update: The project is progressing. Several workstreams are underway and we are addressing various issues as they arise. Stakeholders have been engaged. We expect continued progress in the coming period and will provide further updates as things develop.”

    After“Status update: The project is at risk of delay. Several workstreams are underway and we are addressing various issues as they arise. Stakeholders have been engaged. We expect continued progress in the coming period and will provide further updates as things develop.”

    The line the readers kept named the risk her goal named: “The project is at risk.” The note itself never said it, and every score was 1 or 2 of 10.

    1 line changed. The rest stayed the same. The fix asked for one more line, 'I need your help this week.' The rewrite never added it.

    A hard text to a friend

    The one fix“Say what happened last weekend to show why you want to talk.”

    Read the full check

    Something went wrong last weekend. The text never says what.

    Before“Hey, I need to talk about something. I have been feeling kind of off about how things went last weekend and I do not want it to be weird. You matter to me and I value our friendship a lot. Can we talk sometime?”

    Readers kept the hurt, not the reason: “Friend hurt last weekend.” Feeling scored 7 of 10. The scene scored 4.

    A toast with no scene

    The one fix“Name one thing Sarah did that was great. Cut the rest.”

    Read the full check

    A birthday toast for Sarah, warm from start to finish, and not one thing she ever did.

    Before“I just want to say thank you to everyone for being here. Sarah is a wonderful person and a great friend to all of us. We are so lucky to know her. Here is to many more years of happiness and good times together.”

    All three readers kept the same line: “Thanks to all for being here.” Not one kept a thing about Sarah. Stories scored 1 of 10.

    THE SITE'S AI

    Hi, I'm this site's AI assistant. Paste words you are about to send and I will run the real checker on them right here, the same three cold readers as the checker button, then ask me follow-ups: the weakest part, or the one fix. I also answer from the site's own pages and its published checks. Nothing you type is saved on the server; this page holds our conversation only while it is open.

    How it works

    1. Three cold readers score your words. They never see each other's notes.

    2. You get what they'll hear first, then other ways to say it. If the readers hit a gap only you can fill, they ask, and your answers sharpen a second set.

    3. One fix, shown on your words. The six scores sit last, in full one tap down. You decide what to send.

    Method and sources

    The SUCCESs checklist comes from Made to Stick, by Chip Heath and Dan Heath, backed by Tversky and Shafir (1992), Loewenstein (1994), and Paivio (1971).

    We test this tool against plain AI: the same ten messages, the same six-part rubric for both sides, judged blind. Across three runs the panel won 7, 5, and 7 of 10.
