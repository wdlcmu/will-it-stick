How to make a message stick

Say one thing. Say it in things you can touch. Leave one gap. End with a scene.

The four moves

1. One core. Pick the one line they should keep, before you write the rest.

2. Concrete words. Swap each big word for a thing, a place, or a time.

3. One gap. Open a question your reader wants closed, and answer it one beat late.

4. One scene. End on a moment they can picture.

A real check

A real panel run: a garden club note whose ask sat buried mid-note.

Before

…we need more volunteers and also the budget is tight… dues are going up slightly and the park cleanup is in May…

After

The garden club will meet on Tuesday and we need help clean the park in May. The budget is tight this year which reminds me dues are going up slightly and last year we planted a lot of flowers near the library and the mayor came and everyone should bring gloves.

The one fix the panel named: “Help us clean the park in May. Sign up to volunteer.”

Real product output, kept as written, grammar slip and all.

What readers heard

“We need volunteers for the park cleanup.” One core, moved up front. Two lines changed; the rest is lightly trimmed.

Why this works

These moves come from the SUCCESs checklist in Made to Stick, by Chip Heath and Dan Heath. This site is independent; the authors did not endorse it.
