Six small starts

Shapes to start from. Not your text.

Each check hands back six shapes: starts, not a rewrite. You pick what fits and write the rest.

Each sample below came from a real run.

1. The proverb

Your point in one short line a friend can repeat. Long is not this shape.

“Fix it free, save money.”

From the repair night run. See Examples.

2. The gap opener

A first line that withholds one fact and opens a question. The next line pays it off.

“What happens on first Saturdays?”

From the repair night run. See Examples.

3. The story: a challenge

A small plot of three or four lines: a person hits a block, acts, and it ends. Your facts only; add none.

“The project faced many problems. We worked hard to fix them. Still, we need help to stay on track.”

From the work note run. See Examples.

4. The story: a bond

A small plot of three or four lines: two people meet, and a bond forms. Your facts only; add none.

“We fix things. You bring a lamp. We fix it free. You get coffee.”

From the repair night run. See Examples.

5. The story: a new spark

A small plot of three or four lines: a spark hits, and a new thought forms. Your facts only; add none.

“We make new plans. We use best practices. We grow.”

From the work memo sample in the tool. Flat words make flat tales.

6. Concrete swaps

Vague words hide the point. A swap trades them for a scene you can see and keep.

“cross-functional synergies” → “teams work together”

From the work memo sample in the tool. The swap is plainer, not grander.

What you do with them

Pick one shape and start from it. We don't write the rest for you; you stay the author.
